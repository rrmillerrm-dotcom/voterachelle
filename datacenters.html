Beat Back the AI Data Center Expansion.

What Is Happening

No buildout for billionaires. Here is what we know.

In May 2026, Avista disclosed in a federal securities filing that it had signed a nonbinding agreement with an unnamed corporation seeking up to 500 megawatts of electricity by 2032. That is equal to more than half the power used by every home and business in Spokane County. The public found out through a securities filing, not a public hearing. Community members organized immediately. The Spokane City Council passed a one-year emergency moratorium on data centers exceeding 25 megawatts. Avista paused negotiations.

Then more came out.

Source: Spokesman-Review, June 3, 2026 · Spokesman-Review, June 12, 2026
Al French and Apricus Energy Partners

A commissioner had secret discussions for over a year.

While the public was only learning about the Avista deal in June 2026, Commissioner Al French had already been meeting privately for more than a year with Apricus Energy Partners, a company proposing to build a massive AI data center on 514 acres on the West Plains at 11703 W. Medical Lake Highway. The proposed facility could eventually use up to 1,000 megawatts of power, nearly the entire output of Avista's Washington and Idaho service area combined.

French did not disclose these discussions while the commission was publicly debating a moratorium on a separate unnamed company's deal with Avista. When asked, he said he felt no obligation to bring it up. "You could have just asked me. I'm not shy about it," he said.

Apricus Energy Partners was formed three years ago. It has fewer than 11 employees. It has no record of ever having built anything. Its founder originally incorporated the company in Arizona in 2023 and later listed a Spokane P.O. Box as its address. The documents revealing the West Plains location were uncovered not by a reporter or a government agency, but by a Spokane County resident who filed a public records request.

Source: Spokesman-Review, July 24, 2026 · Spokesman-Review, August 2, 2026
The West Plains

A community already fighting for clean water.

The West Plains is not an abstract location on a map. It is a community of families, farms, and small operations whose well water has been contaminated with PFAS, so-called forever chemicals, from firefighting foam used at Fairchild Air Force Base and Spokane International Airport. The EPA tested 411 private wells in the area in 2024 and found that 57% contained PFAS levels exceeding safe drinking water standards. Residents have been dealing with this crisis since 2017.
